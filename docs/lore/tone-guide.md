# Tone Guide

Rules for all player-facing text in Broken Divinity: dialogue, descriptions, item text, UI messages, lore fragments, and flavor text. Consistency of voice is what makes the world feel real.

## Voice

### Restrained Horror
Show, don't tell. Describe what the character **sees and feels** — not what the narrator thinks is scary. The player's imagination is the best horror tool.

- Good: *"The wall is warm. It shouldn't be warm."*
- Bad: *"A terrifying wall of living flesh pulses before you with eldritch horror!"*

### Pragmatic Language
Characters speak like **survivors**, not fantasy heroes. Short sentences. Practical concerns. Nobody monologues when they're hungry.

- Good: *"Three days of water left. Maybe four if we ration."*
- Bad: *"We must steel ourselves against the coming darkness, for our supplies dwindle!"*

### Religious Weight
Faith language carries **real power** in this world. Angels exist. Demons exist. Prayer might actually do something. Use religious language deliberately, never casually or for flavor.

- Good: *"She prayed before entering the Cathedral. Not out of habit — out of genuine fear of what might answer."*
- Bad: *"Holy moly, that was a close one!"*

### Science-Adjacent (Thaumaturgy)
Thaumaturgy descriptions sound like **field engineering**, not spell-casting. Equipment calibrates, overloads, and malfunctions. Energy has measurable properties.

| Use | Don't Use |
|-----|-----------|
| "Calibrate the conduit array" | "Channel the arcane energies" |
| "Thaumic levels are spiking" | "The magical power grows" |
| "Equipment malfunction" | "The spell went wrong" |
| "Residual contamination" | "Lingering enchantment" |
| "Signal interference" | "Mystical disruption" |

### Grounded Perspective
The narrator is **not omniscient**. Description comes from what a human in this world would perceive and understand. The player character doesn't know cosmic truth — they know what they can see, hear, smell, and feel.

## Do

- **Use sensory details**: Smell of sulfur, metallic taste of Celestial exposure, buzzing of thaumic equipment, cold sweat, trembling hands
- **Let silence and absence create dread**: What's missing is scarier than what's present. An empty room that should have people in it. A sound that stops.
- **Give enemies personality through behavior, not monologues**: A demon that watches you from across the room before attacking is scarier than one that explains its motivation
- **Reference the pre-Sundering world as context**: This used to be a hospital. That was a highway. The normalcy of the past makes the present more disturbing.
- **Use concrete numbers and specifics**: "Three rounds of ammunition" not "dwindling supplies." "The wall is 2 meters thick" not "an imposing wall."
- **Let faction bias color information**: What a Puritan tells you about demons is different from what an Occultist tells you. Neither is lying — both believe their version.

## Don't

- **Use "eldritch," "arcane," "mystic," or generic fantasy vocabulary**: These words are drained of meaning. Use specific, concrete description instead.
- **Make angels good or demons purely evil**: Both are alien and self-interested. Angels enforce protocol that may kill you. Demons offer deals that may benefit you.
- **Write villain monologues**: Enemies act, they don't explain. A demon might negotiate, but it won't lecture.
- **Reference post-1993 technology or culture**: The Sundering happened in 1993. No smartphones, no internet, no post-1993 pop culture. Technology froze.
- **Use "chosen one" narratives**: The player is a survivor, not a hero. No prophecies. No destiny. Just choices and consequences.
- **Explain the horror**: If you have to tell the player something is scary, it isn't. Describe the evidence and let them conclude.
- **Use exclamation marks in descriptive text**: Restraint. Exclamation marks are for dialogue only, and sparingly.

## Vocabulary Reference

### Forbidden Words
These words are **banned** from all game text:

| Word | Replacement |
|------|-------------|
| Eldritch | *(describe the specific wrongness)* |
| Arcane | Thaumic, anomalous |
| Mystic / Mystical | Unexplained, anomalous |
| Spell / Spellcast | Thaumic operation, procedure |
| Mana | Thaumic energy, charge |
| Enchanted | Modified, treated, conduit-wired |
| Wizard / Mage / Sorcerer | Thaumaturge, operator, researcher |
| Quest | Job, task, request, lead |
| Hero / Heroic | *(remove — describe the action instead)* |
| Evil | *(describe the specific harm)* |

## Dialogue Patterns by Species

### Human
Natural, colloquial, varied by background. Contractions, slang (period-appropriate — pre-1993), incomplete sentences. Different registers by faction.

### Angelic
Formal, protocol-laden, rank-dependent. See [species.md](species.md) for the full communication table. Hebrew terms used naturally, not translated.

### Demonic
Type-dependent — ranges from bestial to eloquent. See [species.md](species.md) for the full communication table. The most dangerous demons are the most articulate.

## Message Log Style

The message log is the player's primary narrative channel. Messages should be:
- **Short**: One sentence preferred. Two maximum.
- **Specific**: "The Shomer strikes you for 12 physical damage" not "An angel attacks!"
- **Neutral**: The log reports events, it doesn't editorialize. "Your sanity drops to 47%" not "You feel yourself slipping into madness!"

**Exception**: At high sanity erosion, the log itself becomes unreliable. False messages, distorted reports, messages that didn't happen. This is the one place where the neutral voice deliberately breaks.
