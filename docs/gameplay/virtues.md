# Virtues

Virtues are the root layer of character identity in Broken Divinity. They are not a cosmetic rename of generic fantasy attributes. They define how a person withstands corruption, interprets obligation, survives pressure, and becomes legible to a world whose theology is broken but still active.

They are the human philosophical layer of progression.

## What Virtues Are For in Play

Virtues should do three jobs in play:

- describe the player's moral and psychological posture under pressure
- give supernatural, social, and survival systems a shared language for who the character is becoming
- shape the meaning of practical proficiencies so growth stays tied to character identity rather than drifting into a generic stat stack

Virtues therefore reach beyond combat numbers. They should influence resistance, faction response, narrative gating, risk tolerance, choice framing, and the tone of the player's victories.

## The Six Core Virtues

| Virtue | Core Meaning | Gameplay Direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Temperance** | Restraint, discipline, refusal of appetite | Governs self-command under temptation, corruption, panic, possession, and reckless power use. Supports controlled action rather than explosive action. |
| **Justice** | Oath, judgment, obligation, moral weight | Governs covenant, authority, promises, rulings, and how seriously the world treats the character's commitments. Supports faction trust, binding choices, and consequence-heavy social play. |
| **Prudence** | Foresight, discernment, interpretation | Governs planning, reading patterns, understanding danger, and acting with a long view. Supports investigation, route choice, ritual caution, and informed preparation. |
| **Fortitude** | Endurance, pain tolerance, refusal to break | Governs persistence through injury, deprivation, siege, and despair. Supports attrition play, survival pressure, and staying functional when the world should have crushed you. |
| **Thumos** | Spirit, wrath, honor-drive, battle-fire | Governs decisive action, courage, zeal, intimidation, and the emotional force that carries someone into danger. Supports heroic pressure, duels, morale swings, and moments of violent resolve. |
| **Metis** | Cunning, improvisation, adaptability | Governs trickery, indirect action, opportunism, and practical intelligence under unstable conditions. Supports ambush, escape, deception, traps, and dirty survival. |

The system is strongest when the same problem can be solved through different virtues. Virtues should create distinct approaches to danger rather than just assign passive bonuses to familiar verbs.

## Kleos: Earned Mythic Significance

Kleos is not a starting virtue and should not behave like a normal attribute. It is earned mythic significance: the degree to which deeds become remembered, retold, feared, admired, or disputed.

Kleos should grow through public action, witnessed sacrifice, infamous betrayal, impossible survival, major victories, and other turning points that alter how factions and powers speak about the player. It measures not only what the character did, but what the world believes the deed meant.

In play, Kleos is the bridge between personal identity and public myth. It should drive recognition, narrative weight, mythic opportunities, recruitment pull, and unwanted attention from powers that notice emerging legends.

## Tension Pairs

Virtues should create internal drama rather than point toward a single ideal build. These tensions are not morality sliders. They are fault lines that help the game express who the character becomes under pressure.

| Tension | Design Use |
| --- | --- |
| **Thumos vs Temperance** | Zeal against restraint. Useful for distinguishing the disciplined champion from the reckless avenger and for making power feel costly when emotion outruns control. |
| **Prudence vs Metis** | Planning against improvisation. Useful for separating the reader of patterns from the opportunistic survivor and for letting preparation and cunning solve different kinds of danger. |
| **Justice vs Kleos** | Duty against legend. Useful for asking whether the player is serving an obligation, building a name, or sacrificing one for the other. |
| **Fortitude vs Temperance** | Bodily endurance against spiritual endurance. Useful for distinguishing the character who can survive pain from the one who can survive corruption. |

These tensions matter because a virtue system loses its point if every strong character converges toward the same balanced profile. Broken Divinity should reward meaningful asymmetry.

## Angelic and Demonic Readings

The same virtues should not read the same way across the setting. Their meaning is filtered through rival cosmologies, which keeps the system rooted in Broken Divinity rather than generic metaphysics.

| Virtue | Angelic Interpretation | Demonic Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Temperance** | Purity, restraint, resistance to temptation | Refusal of appetite, resistance to bargain, mastery over hunger |
| **Justice** | Covenant, judgment, rightful order | Debt, vengeance, blood-price, enforced hierarchy |
| **Prudence** | Discernment, prophecy, wise interpretation | Omens, hidden patterns, dangerous knowing |
| **Fortitude** | Trial, martyrdom, endurance in suffering | Survival under curse, famine, plague, and ruin |
| **Thumos** | Righteous zeal, holy wrath | Blood-fury, kingly violence, domination |
| **Metis** | Serpent-wisdom, cunning counsel, indirect obedience | Contract loopholes, trickery, survival by wit |
| **Kleos** | A name remembered among the living and weighed by witness | A name cut into memory through fear, debt, or enduring infamy |

This split should appear in faction rhetoric, rite language, quest framing, companion judgment, and the kinds of rewards different powers offer for the same underlying trait.

## Hybrid Progression Direction

Virtues should be the root layer of progression. They explain what sort of person the player is becoming. Concrete proficiencies should remain only where they represent practical, teachable competence rather than abstract human traits.

Keep proficiencies when they answer a practical question:

- can this character use a weapon, repair a machine, provide first aid, or move quietly because they are trained to do so
- does this activity depend on tools, repetition, or explicit technique
- can two characters share similar virtues but still differ meaningfully because one has the relevant training and the other does not

Do not keep proficiencies when they merely restate a virtue in narrower terms. If a skill is just another way of asking who resists pressure, who reads intent, who commands respect, or who outwits danger, the root system should stay with virtues.

The direction is hybrid, but not additive. Broken Divinity should not stack a full abstract skill sheet on top of virtues. Practical proficiencies remain where they are materially useful. Virtues provide the interpretive foundation that gives those proficiencies meaning.

## Design Constraints and Anti-Goals

- Do not turn virtues into a straight rename of Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma.
- Do not give every virtue a neat combat-only lane that collapses the system back into attack, defense, speed, and damage.
- Do not duplicate virtues with a second layer of abstract skills. If a skill is not materially practical, it probably belongs in the virtue layer instead.
- Do not treat virtues as a simple good-versus-evil meter. Angels, demons, and humans can all value the same virtue for different reasons.
- Do not make Kleos a normal XP total or a passive fame bar. It should be mythic, public, and partially outside direct player control.
- Do not chase perfect symmetry. Uneven, situational, and tension-driven expression is healthier than six equally weighted number buckets.
- Do not let optimization erase identity. The design goal is to ask what kind of soul survives this world, not just which stat line produces the highest output.

If the system is working, a character should be understandable as a kind of soul before they are understandable as a build.
